Stop making
logos. Start
making decisions.

A logo is a symptom, not a solution. Here's the work that has to come before the designer opens Illustrator.

M
Mike Moreno
Brand Strategist · Mike the Rebel

The first thing most founders do when they decide their brand isn't working is brief a designer for a new logo. New logo, new website, maybe a fresh colour palette. Done.

I understand the impulse. Visual work feels tangible. You can point at it, share it, get feedback on it. Strategy is messier. It lives in documents and conversations and half-formed thoughts. A logo is a thing you can hold.

But here's what I've observed across hundreds of brand projects: founders who lead with the logo almost always end up redoing it within two years.

"A logo is just a flag. The question is: what country does it represent?"

What a logo actually does

A logo is a recognition device. Its job is to help people identify your brand quickly and consistently across contexts. That's it. It doesn't tell your story. It doesn't communicate your value. It doesn't position you against competitors.

All of that work has to be done before a single vector is drawn.

Decisions first.
Design second.

The Rebel design sequence · Strategy before aesthetics

The decisions that actually matter

Before any visual work, here are the decisions that need to be made:

Pre-design decisions checklist

  • Position: What specific space does your brand occupy that no-one else owns?
  • Audience: Who specifically are you for, and who are you consciously not for?
  • Voice: How do you speak, and how don't you? What's the tone when you're at your best?
  • Promise: What do you guarantee the people you work with? What can they count on?
  • Personality: If your brand were a person at a dinner party, how would it show up?

These aren't design questions. They're business questions. And without answers to them, the designer is making aesthetic guesses. Good design based on wrong strategy is still wrong.

Why the logo first approach fails

When you start with a logo, you're working backwards. The designer tries to visualise something that hasn't been defined yet. You end up in revision loops: "I like it but something's off," because the visual is searching for a strategy that doesn't exist yet.

Even when the logo looks great in isolation, it doesn't land properly in the market because it's not anchored to anything real. It's an aesthetic. Not a brand.

Aesthetics vs. Brand · Two very different things

What to do instead

Run the strategy first. Even if it's rough. Even if it's just a Google Doc with bullet points. Get the positioning, the audience, the voice, and the promise documented before you brief anyone on visual work.

Then take that document to a designer. Watch how differently the process unfolds. The brief is clearer. The concepts are more focused. The revisions are fewer. And the final result actually works — not just aesthetically, but commercially.

"Give a designer a vibe. Get a logo. Give a designer a strategy. Get a brand."

The logo is the last decision, not the first. It's the visual expression of everything that came before it. When you build it that way, it works. When you don't, you'll be back in two years starting over.
